A short primer

Six things to understand before you go anywhere near China.

Written for Western brands weighing up the market for the first time. It isn't everything — but every brand we have seen struggle in China misjudged at least one of these. The good news: it has never been cheaper to find out where you stand before you commit.

Your name — and your trademark — come first

Your brand will have a Chinese name whether you choose one or not: consumers, distributors and the press will coin one for you. A good Chinese name carries your meaning, sounds right in Mandarin, and avoids unfortunate connotations in its characters. Coca-Cola's 可口可乐 (“tasty fun”) is the model; plenty of well-known brands have got it wrong.

More urgent still: China is a first-to-file trademark jurisdiction. Whoever registers a name first owns it, regardless of who used it first elsewhere. Brands routinely arrive to find their own name — Latin and Chinese — already registered by a squatter. Register early, in both scripts, in every relevant class.

How ArcOn helps: naming strategy, character selection, and trademark filing before anything is public.

Choose your entry route deliberately

There are several ways in, each with different cost, control and speed. Cross-border e-commerce lets you sell from overseas via platforms like Tmall Global without a Chinese entity or full product registration — the quickest way to test demand. General trade means importing, registering products and selling domestically, with more compliance but full market access. A distributor or partner lowers your risk and your control in equal measure. Many brands start with one route and graduate to another.

How ArcOn helps: market analysis and a four-year plan that says which route, in what order, and what it will cost.

The channels are not the ones you know

There is no Amazon, Instagram or Google. Commerce runs through Tmall and JD, and increasingly through Douyin (China's TikTok) livestreams, where a host can sell more in an evening than a store does in a quarter. Discovery happens on Xiaohongshu (RED) and WeChat — and, increasingly, through AI assistants that summarise what the internet thinks of your brand before a consumer ever sees your page. Each platform has its own rules, costs and audience, and consumer expectations — same-day delivery, live engagement, constant novelty — are higher than in the West.

How ArcOn helps: storefront set-up and operation, platform marketing, and a WeChat presence built in-house.

Localise the offer, not just the label

Gifting seasons (Chinese New Year, Mid-Autumn, 11.11), pack sizes, price ladders, flavour and colour preferences, and the story a brand tells about its heritage all differ. Premium brands succeed when they keep their authenticity and adapt how it is expressed — not by becoming Chinese, and not by ignoring the market.

How ArcOn helps: positioning, product and pricing decisions made by a team that has launched premium brands here before.

Compliance is real and it is slow

Product registration, labelling, import licences, food and cosmetics testing, data rules, and the mechanics of setting up and running a Chinese entity all take longer than first-timers expect. None of it is optional and all of it needs to be sequenced into the plan from the beginning.

How ArcOn helps: corporate establishment, regulatory compliance and finance run by a Hong Kong-based team with 30 years of corporate services experience.

Plan in years, budget in years

China rewards patience and punishes half-commitment. The brands that build lasting businesses treat the first two years as investment, measure against a multi-year plan, and have someone accountable on the ground. The ones that treat it as a quick export win usually leave quietly.

How ArcOn helps: a four-year forecast up front, and a team that stays to run the business against it.
